Method

Four passes over the B2B data

I read the live catalogue file the Amit Electricals site builds from — 2,573 products across Polycab (1,998), Surya (270), Halonix (251), Indo (53) and one Rexsun placeholder. Every product carries a brand, a category taken from that brand's own website, specs, MRP where published, and a locally cached image.

Pass 1

Keep only categories a household shops for

Fans, lighting, kitchen and water-heating appliances, garment care, torches, and the plug-and-holder accessories people replace themselves. Everything sold through an electrician or a project quote is dropped at this step.

Rebuild the taxonomy for shoppers, not for brands

The source categories come from four different manufacturers' websites and contradict each other. Indo files geysers under "Iron"; Surya files water heaters under "Heating Appliances"; air coolers hide inside "General". I remapped all 268 onto one shopper taxonomy: 10 departments, 26 categories.

The one decision that shapes everything

677 trade SKUs become 268 product pages

This is the single biggest difference between the B2B site and this one, and it is worth being explicit about because it changes how the whole store is built. On the dealer site, "Pearl COB Swivel LED Downlight · 12 Wattage · 4000K" is a line item you order by code. On a consumer site it is one product page with a wattage selector and a light-colour selector, and the price updates as you choose.

Same for fans: SILENCIO MINI is 28 SKUs in the trade file — sweep sizes × finishes. It becomes one product page with a size and a colour swatch. The customer sees a clean, shoppable store; every underlying SKU is still there, mapped behind the variant picker, so stock and pricing stay accurate.

What this buys you

A shopper browsing ceiling fans sees 55 fans, not 256 near-identical rows. Search results stay clean. Category pages load fast. And when you add a finish or a wattage later, it's a variant on an existing page, not a new listing to photograph and describe from scratch.

Category mix

10 departments, 26 categories, ₹36 to ₹19,999

The mix follows the strength of the underlying catalogue rather than an idealised store plan — fans and lighting are where Polycab and Surya are deep, so that is where the store is deep. Appliances are thinner and are exactly where the Rexsun own-label range fills the gap.

Rexsun own-label

Six hero products carry the brand and the margin

The six creatives you shared — chopper, hand blender, juicer mixer grinder, two mixer grinders, and the 25 L storage water heater — are the only products on this site where you own the brand, the pricing and the warranty. They should not sit quietly inside "Kitchen Appliances" alongside a Surya mixer.

My recommendation: a permanent Rexsun entry in the main navigation, a dedicated brand page built from those six creatives, and a Rexsun badge on the product card wherever one appears in a category listing. Everywhere else on the site, the other four brands are presented plainly, by name — you are the retailer for them and the manufacturer for Rexsun, and the store should read that way.

What I need from you for these six

Selling price and MRP, warranty terms in writing (the creatives say 1 year on the chopper and the JMG), box contents, and the product shots as separate files — the creatives are marketing banners with text baked in, and a product page needs the appliance on white with three or four angles.

What I cut

2,305 SKUs stay on the B2B site

Kept, and why

  • Ceiling, table, pedestal, wall and exhaust fans — the single largest online electrical category in India, and Polycab's range here is genuinely competitive.
  • LED bulbs, battens, panels, downlights — low ticket, high repeat, ships flat. Good for first orders and for average basket size.
  • Geysers, immersion rods, room heaters, coolers — seasonal spikes you can plan campaigns around.
  • Mixers, kettles, induction, irons, air fryers — where Rexsun's own range sits, so the category has to exist.
  • Spike guards, multiplugs, holders, ceiling roses, doorbells — genuine self-install items and useful basket fillers.

Cut, and why

  • Switchgear — 492 SKUs (MCB, RCBO, RCCB, distribution boards). Chosen by an electrician against a load schedule, not by a homeowner. Wrong selection is a safety issue.
  • House wire — 248 SKUs. Sold in 90 m and 180 m coils at ₹2,700–₹45,100. That is a contractor purchase.
  • Modular switches and plates — 495 SKUs. Bought as a whole-house set with an electrician, and returns on the wrong plate size are painful. Worth revisiting later as curated room kits.
  • Solar grid-tie inverters — 42 SKUs up to ₹8.67 lakh. Site survey, subsidy paperwork, installation. Not a cart item.
  • Commercial and industrial luminaires, street and flood lights, hospitality modules. Specified by consultants.
  • Air circulators and heavy-duty farrata fans at ₹11,000–₹18,000 — factory equipment.

Data gaps

Four things that block a launch, in priority order

01

41 of 268 products have no published price

Halonix publishes no MRP at all, and Surya publishes it only on some ranges. The affected lines are marked price TBD in the list above — heaviest in mixer grinders, Surya water heaters and air coolers. Either you give me a price list for these, or I drop them from launch and add them in phase two. They cannot go live without a price.

02

Product images are manufacturer catalogue shots

2,524 of the 2,573 products have a cached image, so coverage is not the problem — quality and rights are. They are scraped from Polycab, Surya and Halonix websites. For a consumer store you need written permission from each brand to use their product photography, which is normally routine for an authorised dealer but has to be asked for. Rexsun's own six need proper shoots regardless.

03

No descriptive copy worth reading

The trade file carries spec tables — sweep, wattage, RPM, air delivery — and almost no consumer-facing description. Every one of the 268 needs a short benefit-led paragraph and three or four bullet points. I can draft all of them from the specs; you review the ones that make claims.

04

Nothing in the data says what is deliverable

A 25 L geyser and a ₹36 lamp holder cannot ship the same way. Every product needs a weight, a box size, and a flag for whether it needs installation. This determines shipping charges, so it has to be settled before checkout is built.

Site features

Nine things the store does. Nothing else.

You asked for simple, and simple is also correct here — a first-time visitor buying a ceiling fan should never meet a feature they have to learn. Everything below earns its place by removing a reason not to buy.

Core

Shop by room, not by department

Bathroom, kitchen, bedroom, living room, outdoor. Most people do not arrive thinking "exhaust fan" — they think "the bathroom needs sorting". The department nav stays for people who know what they want.

Core

One page per product, variants inside it

Size, finish, wattage and light colour are pickers on the page. Price, image and stock update as you choose. This is the variant merge, made visible.

Core

Filters that match how people decide

Room size for fans, brightness in watts for lights, litres for geysers, price band, brand, BLDC or induction motor. Not a wall of 30 spec checkboxes.

Differentiator

"Which size do I need?" helper

Three questions on fan and geyser pages — room dimensions, ceiling height, how many people bathe — and it recommends a sweep or a litre capacity. This is the single most common pre-purchase question in this category and nobody answers it well.

Differentiator

Running-cost line on every fan and light

"About ₹340 a year at 8 hours a day." It makes the case for a BLDC fan at ₹6,099 over an induction fan at ₹2,799 far better than a spec table does, and it is honest arithmetic from the wattage you already hold.

Differentiator

Warranty and service, stated plainly

Warranty period on the product card, and a service page that says who to call. In this category trust is the whole purchase decision, and most competing sites bury it.

Checkout

Guest checkout, three fields, UPI first

Phone, address, pay. Account creation offered after the order, never before it. UPI at the top of the payment list because that is what the majority will use.

Checkout

Delivery date before payment

Pincode entered on the product page returns a real date and any installation charge, so nothing changes at the last step.

Post-order

Order tracking on a phone number

No login. Enter the number you ordered with, see where it is. Plus WhatsApp updates at dispatch and delivery.

Deliberately not building

No order pad. No bulk-quantity grid. No dealer login, tier pricing, GSTIN capture, quotation flow, or enquiry cart — every one of those is an Amit Electricals feature and every one of them would confuse a consumer. Also skipping, for v1: wishlists, reviews (nothing to show on day one), loyalty points, and a chatbot.

UI / UX

Five screens, and a rule for each

Screen 1
Home

Rexsun's own six above the fold, then shop-by-room tiles, then the seasonal category. A shopper reaches a product in two taps.

Screen 2
Category

Filters in a left rail on desktop, one sheet on mobile. Cards show image, name, price, warranty, key spec. Nothing else.

Screen 3
Product

Gallery, price, variant pickers, pincode and date, Add to cart. Specs, running cost and warranty below, in that order.

Screen 4
Cart & checkout

One page. Items, address, payment, total. No steps, no progress bar, no upsell interruptions.

Screen 5
Confirmation

Order number, delivery date, WhatsApp opt-in, and a tracking link that needs no password.

The visual direction takes its cue from the creatives you already have: Rexsun's red and gold on clean white, with heavy sans headlines and a lot of daylight around the product. Photography-led, not illustration-led. Mobile is the primary design target — assume three-quarters of traffic and nearly all first visits arrive on a phone, and design the desktop layout up from that.

Two rules I would like to hold to throughout: every price on screen is the price you pay — shipping and installation stated on the product page, never revealed at checkout; and no interruption before the cart — no newsletter overlay, no spin-to-win, no exit popup. In a trust-led category those cost more orders than they capture emails.

Build plan

Same stack as Amit Electricals, different shape

Next.js with the catalogue as a build-time file, exactly as the B2B site works — 268 products is small, so category and product pages render instantly with no database round trip. The database holds only what actually changes: orders, addresses, stock and price overrides. Razorpay for payments, which covers UPI, cards, net banking and the wallets, and supports the refund flow you will need. The site runs on port 3690 locally, alongside your other projects.

Sequence: catalogue import and taxonomy first, then the browse and product pages, then cart and Razorpay, then the order and tracking flow, then the Rexsun brand pages and the two calculators. The store is a working shop by the end of the third stage; everything after that is polish.

Your decisions

Six answers and I start building

01

Is 268 the right number, or should I trim to 200?

I can cut the thinnest 68 — mostly single-variant Surya lighting lines — and launch tighter. My recommendation is to keep 268: the depth is in fans and lighting, which is where the searches are.

02

The 41 unpriced products — price list, or drop them?

Includes almost the whole Surya mixer grinder range and the Surya water heaters. If Rexsun's own mixers are covering that shelf anyway, dropping them is defensible.

03

Selling price against MRP

The list above is MRP. Do you want a flat discount across the store, a margin rule per category, or a price set line by line? This decides whether I build a pricing rule or a price column.

04

Where do you deliver, and who installs?

Tricity only, North India, or pan-India through a courier? And is geyser and ceiling-fan installation something you offer, charge for, or leave to the customer? This changes the product page, not just the shipping table.

05

Rexsun's six: prices, warranty text, and photography

Nothing about the own-label range can go live without these, and they are the pages that matter most.

06

Do the other four brands appear by name?

I have assumed yes — Polycab, Surya, Halonix and Indo shown plainly as the brands you retail, with Rexsun as the house label. Confirm, because it changes the navigation and the whole trust story.
